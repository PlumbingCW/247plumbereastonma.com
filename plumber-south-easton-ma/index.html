Plumber in South Easton, Easton, MA

South Easton is a corridor village. Washington Street, which carries Route 138, ties together mill-era cottages, small businesses and newer homes, so one street can hold several kinds of plumbing.

A village built along a road

South Easton has its own ZIP, 02375, and sits south of North Easton along Washington Street. The state historic survey says settlement began around 1695 along that corridor, which later became a mix of commercial, industrial and residential uses between Belmont Street and the South Easton depot.

Early mills and forges stood near the junction of Route 138 and Depot Street. Cotton thread mills followed in the early 19th century, then the Easton Machine Company. The Morse thread operations also drew workers, and the town's preservation plan says the company bought houses in South Easton and Eastondale for them.

What the houses look like

The survey names these housing types. They are a guide to the village, not a list of every street.

Federal cottages at the corners

Small cottages stand near places the survey calls Daley's Corner and Alger's Corner. A Colonial Revival house stands at Morse Corner.

Workers' housing along Central Street

Early 20th century workers' housing runs along Central Street toward Morris Corner.

Commercial buildings on the main road

Late industrial commercial buildings, mostly frame and a story and a half, line parts of Washington Street.

Newer homes have been added over time, so many streets mix eras. Treat these descriptions as background and let the actual pipes in your house decide the work.

Homes and small businesses on the same road

Because Washington Street is a working road, some South Easton calls come from shops, restaurants and offices as well as houses. A restaurant has grease to manage and a storefront may have a floor drain or a backflow device. Those jobs differ from a household sink, and the plumber needs to know which you have.

If the call is for a business, read the commercial plumbing page. If it is for a house, drain cleaning and water heater repair cover most first calls.

Water, sewer and what to ask

South Easton is on the same groundwater system as the rest of town, run by the Easton Water Division. Its 2024 report describes treatment with lime and potassium hydroxide for corrosion control and ultraviolet disinfection at every well. It also lists new treatment facilities on Washington Street at two wells. The report gives no hardness figure.

We do not know which South Easton streets are on town sewer and which use septic. A 2013 news report lists Five Corners among areas in the town's wastewater plan, but plans change. Ask the town or check your closing records, and tell the plumber what you find.

Permits and verifying a plumber

For a shop or a house, the rule is the same. Under 248 CMR, the state plumbing code, a licensed plumber pulls a permit from the local inspector for most installs and replacements. Section 3.05 exempts faucet or valve leak repairs and clearing a stoppage.

Check any plumber's license through the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters before work starts.
