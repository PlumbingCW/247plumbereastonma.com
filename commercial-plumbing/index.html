Commercial plumbing is not residential plumbing at a larger scale

Commercial buildings have longer pipe runs, bigger fixtures, more simultaneous users and tighter code obligations. A restaurant dishwasher, a retail restroom core and an office break room each stress a system differently.

The consequences are different too. A residential clog costs a morning. A commercial one can close a kitchen, fail a health inspection or send customers home. Good commercial plumbing is partly repair and partly planning the work around your hours.

What to do first when a business plumbing line fails

Protect people and stock first. Close off the affected area, put out wet floor signs and move inventory off the floor. If water is near electrical equipment, switch off power only from a dry spot or leave it alone and tell the dispatcher.

Find the main shutoff for the building and any isolation valves for the affected area. Stopping the flow limits damage while you wait. Then call the 24/7 line and describe the type of business, the fixtures out of service and whether the issue is water coming in or coming out.

Grease traps and interceptors

Fats, oils and grease harden inside drain lines and cause a large share of sewer blockages. A grease trap or interceptor catches them before they reach the municipal line or your own septic system, but only if it is cleaned on schedule.

Maintenance intervals vary by device and by local rules, so ask the Easton building department or the sewer authority what applies to your establishment. Whatever the interval, keep a log of pumping and cleaning. Waiting until the trap smells or the line backs up means you are already behind.

Signs your grease trap is overdue

Slow floor drains, a sour smell in the kitchen, grease visible at the cleanout and repeat clogs soon after the line is cleared. Regular service plus hydro jetting of the kitchen line keeps the whole system moving.

Backflow prevention and testing

Backflow devices stop used or contaminated water from flowing back into the public supply. Commercial dishwashers, boilers with chemical treatment, irrigation systems, cooling equipment and beverage dispensers are the kinds of connections that need protection.

Under the Massachusetts cross-connection rules in 310 CMR 22.22, reduced pressure and double check assemblies are tested by a MassDEP certified tester. The state manual calls for semiannual testing of the first type and annual testing of the second. A failed device has to be repaired or replaced and retested within 14 days.

What to do with the report

Test results are recorded on a report form and go to the water supplier. Ask the Easton Water Division how it wants reports delivered, and keep your own copy with the building records.

Storm drain and catch basin cleaning

Parking lots collect sand, leaves, trash and oil. A catch basin that fills up sends water across the pavement and into doors and loading docks. Roof drains and trench drains clog in the same way.

Never rinse mats, wash buckets or kitchen waste into a storm drain. Storm drains run to streams and wetlands untreated, and towns treat dumping as an illicit discharge. For lots that flood, we jet the lines, clear the basins and tell you whether a camera look at the underground pipe is worth doing.

Codes that apply to commercial work

Backflow device testing follows MassDEP's cross-connection rules in 310 CMR 22.22, which set retest intervals by device type. Plumbing work itself falls under the state plumbing code, 248 CMR, which sets the standards for materials and installation, so ask who is pulling the permit for any larger job.
