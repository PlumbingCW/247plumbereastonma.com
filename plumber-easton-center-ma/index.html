Plumber in Easton Center, Easton, MA

Easton Center is the old civic heart of the town, built around the Green where the first settler built in 1694. It is quieter than the mill villages, and its plumbing questions tend to be about age and septic.

The old center of town

Easton Center is a named village near the middle of town. Wikipedia says the first settler, Clement Briggs, built his home on Easton Green in 1694. The preservation plan says town meetings and offices stayed at Easton Center even as North Easton grew.

Sites the plan names include Central Cemetery on Center Street, the Congregational meetinghouse, the old Center School, now a business, and a former town poor farm. A gazetteer places the village less than two miles northeast of Five Corners.

Housing: suburban residences and worker houses

The state historic survey says Easton Green and Depot Street hold mid to late 19th century suburban residences, with late industrial worker housing along Depot and Central Streets. A stone cottage in the Federal and Gothic Revival styles stands on Center Street.

The Old Colony Branch Railroad reached Easton Center by way of Depot Street in 1888. As in any old village, a house here may combine several periods, so what is behind the walls is worth confirming.

Use the age as a guide, not a diagnosis

A century-old house is not a problem by itself. What matters is the condition of the pipe you actually have. Ask the plumber to show you the problem pipe before you agree to a repair or a replacement, and ask for the price before work starts.

Avoid putting off a small leak. A drip behind plaster is cheaper to repair early, and you should document it with a dated photo so an insurer can see when it began.

Water, sewer and an old district history

The preservation plan notes that Easton Center once formed its own water district. Today the Easton Water Division reports one groundwater system with 7,705 active services and more than 170 miles of main, according to its 2024 report. The report gives no hardness figure.

Sewer is an open question. A 2013 news report says the town's wastewater plan named Easton Center as a priority area and hoped to arrange treatment with Brockton. We found no source confirming that this was built. Ask the town or check your records. If you are on septic, tell the plumber first.

Permits and verifying a plumber

Before a water heater or a repipe, ask who is pulling the permit. According to 248 CMR, the Massachusetts plumbing code, that is the licensed plumber, and the local plumbing inspector reviews the work. Under 248 CMR 3.05, a faucet or valve leak repair and clearing a stoppage do not need one.

Verify any plumber's license through the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters before work starts.
