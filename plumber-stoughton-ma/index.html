Water on the floor in Stoughton: first moves

If a pipe has burst or a supply line has let go, find the main shutoff and close it before you do anything else. In most houses it sits where the water line enters, often in the basement near the meter. Then open a cold tap at the lowest sink to drain what is left in the pipes, and call our 24/7 emergency line.

If the problem is a backup instead, stop running water everywhere, including the washing machine. Do not pour chemical drain opener into a line that is already full. Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are slow, because that decides whether we start with drain cleaning at a fixture or the main line.

The emergencies we get called to in 02072

Main line backups

Floor drains that bubble when a toilet flushes, or a tub that fills when the dishwasher runs, point to the main. If the house is on town sewer, the cause may be your lateral or the street main, and finding out which is the first job. Sewer line cleaning clears the house side.

Water heater failures

A tank that leaks from the base, rumbles loudly or gives no hot water is an emergency in a household with children or in January. We handle water heater repair first, and replacement when the tank itself has failed.

Frozen and burst pipes

Pipes in exterior walls and unheated crawlspaces split during New England cold snaps. A split pipe may stay quiet until the thaw, then flood the room. Our burst pipe repair visit stops the water and replaces the damaged section.

Older houses around Stoughton Square and newer homes beyond

Stoughton was settled in 1713 and incorporated in 1726, so the streets around Stoughton Square include some of the oldest housing we serve. Houses of that age tend to have a mix of original and patched plumbing: cast iron or clay in the drain, galvanized steel on old supply runs, and copper added later. Galvanized lines clog from the inside with rust and scale, which shows up as weak flow at upstairs fixtures.

Farther out, postwar capes and ranches and newer subdivisions usually have copper or PEX supply and PVC drains. Those fail differently. A pinhole in a copper line, a failed fitting on a water heater or a washing machine hose is more typical than a collapsed drain. When a leak is hidden, leak detection finds it before we open a wall.

Before the plumber reaches your Stoughton home

A few minutes of preparation help the plumber start work the moment they walk in. None of it needs tools.

Find the main shutoff

Know where the water enters your home. If you cannot close it, tell the dispatcher and we will talk you through it.

Clear the way

Move stored boxes away from the basement stairs, the water heater and any floor drain that is backing up.

Protect what matters

Lift rugs, electronics and paperwork off wet floors. Do not stand in water near outlets or appliances.

Note what you saw

Which fixtures, when it started and any noises. Photos help your insurance claim later.

Keep pets and children out

A flooded basement can hide electrical hazards, so keep them upstairs.

If you smell gas or see sparks, leave the house and call 911 first. A plumber is the second call.

Permits and licensing when work is done in Stoughton

Massachusetts requires a permit from the local plumbing inspector before plumbing is installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired, and only a licensed plumber can pull that permit. Fixing a leak at a faucet or valve and clearing a stoppage are exempt, according to the state plumbing code (248 CMR). A water heater replacement is not exempt, so expect a permit.

For local steps, ask the Stoughton building department or the town's plumbing inspector. You can check any plumber's license on the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters lookup at mass.gov before you let anyone into the house. That applies to us too.
