How the seasons break plumbing in Sharon

Winter is the hard test. Sharon is a Norfolk County town about 17 miles southwest of Boston, and it gets the same freeze-thaw swings as the rest of eastern Massachusetts. A pipe in an exterior wall, a garage ceiling or an unheated crawlspace can freeze on a January night and split quietly, then show itself when the thaw lets water run. If one has burst, shut the main valve and call. Crews handle burst pipe repair at any hour.

Spring brings the second problem, groundwater. Snowmelt and March rain raise the water table, and basements with a sump pit are the first place it shows. A pump that sat idle all winter may jam or fail just when it is needed, so lift the lid and pour in a bucket of water to see it start. If it does not, that is an emergency, and sump pump repair is the fix.

Summer storms and what they do to a Sharon home

Heavy summer rain loads the ground quickly. These are the calls that tend to follow a cloudburst.

Sump pit overflows

A pump can run flat out and still lose to a downpour. A battery backup and a clear discharge line make the difference, so check that the outlet hose is not blocked or frozen to the ground from last winter.

Floor drains that gurgle

When soil is saturated, a main-line root intrusion shows itself fast. Several fixtures slowing together point to the main, and drain cleaning is the first step.

Septic systems under stress

A tank and leach field that cannot drain into wet soil back up into the house. Stop laundry and long showers and call before more water goes in.

Septic first: why Sharon plumbing is different

The town's own septic page describes Sharon homes and businesses as entirely dependent on on-site wastewater processing. In practice that means most houses here have a private tank and leach field instead of a street sewer. Septic work in Sharon runs through the Board of Health and its regulations, and Title 5 (310 CMR 15.000, administered by MassDEP) sets the state rules for inspection.

That changes how we respond to a backup. A slow main drain in a septic house is not always a clog, and running a cable into a full tank solves nothing. We ask whether you are on septic, when the tank was last pumped and where the cleanout is, so the right plumber arrives with the right tools. Indoor plumbing still falls under the state plumbing code, so ask the local inspector what permit applies.

Where we cover in Sharon

Water supply: wells, treatment and what it means for pipes

Sharon Water Department supplies the town from six groundwater wells through about 120 miles of water main, according to its 2025 water quality report. The water is disinfected and treated for corrosion control to reduce lead and copper, and the town reports it is building a new treatment plant for three of its wells to remove PFAS. The report does not give a hardness number, so we do not claim one.

The report also notes that some service lines installed before 1960 may contain lead, and that the town has a replacement program. If your house is that old, ask the Water Department about your service line before any repiping. When a line from the street to your meter is the problem, water line repair is the right page to read.

Roads, rail and getting to you

Route 27 runs through the center of Sharon and leads on toward Route 1 in Walpole, and Interstate 95 crosses the town as well. Sharon is a neighboring town to the north of Easton, so the drive is short. We never promise an exact arrival time, but we do tell you what to do while the plumber is on the way.

The Sharon station on the MBTA Providence/Stoughton Line puts many households on a Boston commute, so the house is often empty for ten hours at a stretch. That is when a slow leak or a failed pump does the most damage. A leak sensor near the water heater and sump pit, plus a trusted neighbor with a key, turns a flood into a short call. Our crews also run leak detection when you suspect water behind a wall.
