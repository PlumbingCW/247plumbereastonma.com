Start with how your household uses hot water

The right heater is the one that fits your habits. A couple that showers at different times needs far less storage than a family where three people shower before school. Count the people, the big fixtures such as a soaking tub, and any simultaneous use like a dishwasher running during a shower.

That count drives capacity and recovery rate, which is how quickly a heater refills with hot water. A tank that is too small runs out, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping extra water warm all day.

Where it goes matters as much as what it is

Clearances are written into the manufacturer's instructions and the code. A gas heater needs combustion air and a clear flue path, while a heat pump heater needs open space so it can draw warmth from the room. Putting either one in a tiny closet can cause trouble that no amount of skill fixes later.

Think about leaks too. A heater above a finished ceiling or next to carpet deserves a drain pan piped to a safe spot. If the heater sits in an Easton garage or crawlspace, winter exposure is a factor, so insulation and freeze protection should be part of the plan.

Safety items every installation should include

These are the details that separate a clean installation from one that causes problems later.

Install safety checklist

  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe
  • Shutoff valves on the cold supply and, for gas, the gas line
  • Flexible connectors or unions that allow service later
  • Proper flue slope and sealed joints on gas units
  • Expansion tank where the plumber finds one is needed
  • Carbon monoxide alarm in the living space for any fuel-burning appliance

If you smell gas at any stage of a project, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 before you call anyone else.

Permits, inspection and the Massachusetts rulebook

Under 248 CMR, plumbing and gas fitting work cannot be installed, altered or replaced until the local inspector issues a permit. An application is made in writing before the work starts, and the inspector is required to act on it within two working days. That acting is not the same as approval, so inspection still follows.

Only licensed individuals can apply. The state's online license lookup lets you check any plumber in a minute, and it is worth doing before you sign anything. If a company suggests skipping the permit, take that as a reason to look elsewhere. Questions about local timing go to the Easton building department.

Local considerations for Easton homeowners

Easton draws its water from town wells, and the Easton Water Division publishes an annual water quality report. Reading it, or testing your own tap, tells you whether mineral buildup is a worry, and that influences how often you will flush the tank. We do not assume a hardness figure for your street.

If you are converting a house from oil-fired hot water to a standalone heater, say so up front. The change can involve chimney work, a new gas or electric supply and a different location, and all of it affects the scope of the job.
