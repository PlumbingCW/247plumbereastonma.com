Boiler Repair in Easton, MA

Carbon monoxide comes first

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Do not go back in to reset the boiler or open windows. Headache, dizziness, nausea and confusion that ease when you leave the house are warning signs too.

Massachusetts law requires working CO alarms in homes with fuel-burning equipment, on each level and near bedrooms. Test yours, and replace batteries on schedule. A boiler can be repaired later, but a person cannot be.

No heat on a freezing night

In New England a dead boiler during a cold snap can mean frozen pipes within a day or two, especially in exterior walls and unheated crawlspaces. Our boiler repair page is a standard service, not an emergency line, but a no-heat call in deep winter is urgent and we treat it that way when we can.

Before you call, check that the thermostat is on and set above the room temperature, the boiler switch is on, and the water pressure gauge reads in the normal range printed on the unit. If the burner will not light, do not keep resetting it. Repeated attempts can flood the combustion chamber with unburned fuel.

Open cabinet doors on exterior walls so room heat reaches the pipes, and let a faucet drip on the coldest wall. These steps buy time while a plumber is on the way.

Hot-water boilers and steam boilers are different machines

Older Easton houses often have one or the other, and the repair path differs.

Hot-water (hydronic) boiler

  • Heats water and pumps it through baseboard, radiators or in-floor loops.
  • A closed loop under pressure, with an expansion tank and circulator pumps.
  • Common faults are air in the lines, a failed circulator or a stuck zone valve.
  • Radiators stay warm to the touch.

Steam boiler

  • Boils water into steam that rises to radiators through the pipes.
  • Runs at low pressure, with a sight glass showing the water level.
  • Common faults are low water, clogged vents, a bad pressuretrol or hammering pipes.
  • Radiators get hot and the pipes can bang.

Steam systems are particular. A plumber who knows them will check the water level and vents before touching anything else.

Boiler problems we see most often

Boiler will not start

No power, a tripped switch, a tripped limit, a failed ignition or a dirty flame sensor.

Strange noises

Kettling from mineral scale, gurgling from trapped air, banging from water hammer in steam lines.

Radiators cold at the top

Air trapped in a hot-water radiator. Bleeding the valve usually fixes it, and if not the circulator may be failing.

Leaks around the boiler

Weeping at a valve, a pressure relief valve discharge or a corroded section. Do not ignore a puddle under the unit.

Pressure too high or too low

A failing expansion tank or a leak somewhere in the loop.

Short cycling

The boiler fires and shuts off within minutes. Often a dirty sensor, an oversized unit or a faulty limit control.

Gas work requires a licensed gas fitter

Anything that touches gas piping, burners, valves or venting must be done by a Massachusetts licensed gas fitter, with a permit pulled by a licensed professional. The Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters licenses both trades. You can check a license on the state lookup before anyone starts.

That rule protects you. A gas leak or a bad vent connection can poison a household without any visible sign. If someone offers to swap a gas valve or adjust the burner without a permit, say no and call somebody else.

Oil-fired boilers follow different rules from gas ones. If you are not sure what applies at your address, ask the Easton building department who should inspect the work.
