Plumber in Eastondale, Easton, MA

Eastondale is the small residential pocket on Easton's eastern edge. Older streets from a trolley and rail era sit beside newer ones, and the Brockton and West Bridgewater lines are close by.

Where Eastondale is

The Easton Historical Society describes Eastondale as nestled between Washington Street and the Brockton and West Bridgewater town lines. Streets it names include Turnpike, High, Pine and Hill, and a newer street called Marisa Drive.

Exact boundaries are not documented, so we describe the area by those anchors and do not draw an edge. The state historic survey places it in the Sequasset area. If your address is near the Brockton line, mention the street and a cross street when you call.

A rail and trolley village that became a quiet neighborhood

Eastondale once had a store, post office, church, social clubs, a railroad depot and trolley service, according to the Historical Society. A rail spur known as the Shovel Handle line ran through it, and a trolley route started in 1897.

The preservation plan adds that workers of the Morse Thread Company lived in houses the company bought in South Easton and Eastondale, and that Eastondale later formed its own water district. The Historical Society describes the neighborhood today as having handsome homes and nicely landscaped vistas.

Older streets and newer ones side by side

Because Marisa Drive is described as newer and the rail-era streets are older, Eastondale mixes generations of construction. Houses of different eras tend to differ in their pipe materials, water heaters and drain lines, so it helps to say how old your house is when you call.

Drain cleaning is the usual first call for slow fixtures, and water heater repair covers the second most common. If a drain keeps backing up, a sewer inspection shows the pipe.

Water and sewer: honest limits

Water comes from the Easton Water Division's groundwater wells. The 2024 report lists lime and potassium hydroxide treatment for corrosion control, ultraviolet disinfection and a plant on Red Mill Road for iron and manganese. It gives no hardness figure.

We could not find a source that says whether Eastondale streets are on town sewer. A 2013 news report describes town plans to discuss wastewater service with Brockton for some Easton areas, but the report does not list Eastondale. Ask the town or check your records, and tell the plumber.

What to tell the plumber about an Eastondale house

Because the streets mix eras and the town line is close, a few details help the first call go well.

Helpful details

  • Your full street address, with a cross street if you are near the Brockton line.
  • Roughly how old the house is, and whether it has additions.
  • Whether the house is on town sewer or septic, if you know.
  • Which fixtures are affected, and whether the problem is steady or comes and goes.

Permits and verifying a plumber

Most plumbing changes need a permit from the local inspector, and under 248 CMR, the state plumbing code, the licensed plumber applies for it. Clearing a stoppage and repairing a faucet or valve leak are exempt, according to 248 CMR 3.05.

Verify any plumber's license through the Massachusetts Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters before work starts.
