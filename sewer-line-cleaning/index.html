How a main line backup shows itself

The main sewer line is the one pipe all your drains share. When it blocks, the signs show up at the lowest point in the house first.

Floor drain or basement tub backs up

The lowest opening is usually where the water appears. If it happens when you run the washing machine or flush a toilet upstairs, the main is full.

Toilets gurgle and fixtures drain slowly together

A single slow sink points to a local clog covered under drain cleaning. When everything is slow at once, look at the main.

Sewage smell or wet spot in the yard

A blockage can force wastewater to find another way out. Odor near a cleanout or a soggy lawn patch is a warning.

What to do first during a main line backup

1

Stop every drop of water use

Skip laundry, dishwashing, showers and flushing. A full line only gets worse.

2

Do not pour chemicals down the drain

Caustic openers rarely move roots or grease and can injure the next person to work on the pipe.

3

Locate your cleanout

A capped pipe in the basement, crawlspace or by the foundation. Point it out when the plumber arrives, but leave the cap on.

Roots, grease and everything else that blocks a main

Tree roots are the leading cause of repeat main line problems in older neighborhoods. They enter through hairline cracks or loose joints seeking moisture and nutrients, then thicken into a net that catches paper and waste. Clay and cast iron pipes with older joints are the easiest targets.

Grease is the other big one. It leaves the kitchen as a warm liquid, cools in the pipe and sticks to the walls, narrowing the pipe a little at a time. Wipes labeled flushable do not break up like paper, and they tangle with roots and grease to form dense plugs.

Town sewer or septic: why the first question matters

Parts of Easton are on town sewer and parts use private septic systems. If your home is on town sewer, the main line runs from the house to a connection near the street, and a blockage in the town's pipe beyond that point is the Easton public works office's concern, not yours. A plumber can tell you from the camera which side of the connection the blockage sits on.

If you are on septic, the pipe runs to a tank. A full tank, a clogged outlet baffle or a failing leach field can produce the same symptoms as a clog, and cabling the line will not help. Tell the dispatcher which system you have so the plumber checks the right thing first.

After the line is open: keeping it that way

Clearing the line is the first step. If the cause was roots, expect them to return within a year or two unless the pipe is repaired, and a camera inspection shows where they get in. If the pipe is cracked or collapsed, cleaning will not hold and sewer line repair is the real fix.

For grease, the habit change is simple. Scrape pans into the trash, wipe them with a paper towel, and keep wipes, feminine products and paper towels out of the toilet. Households with older cast iron lines may want scheduled cleanings, because scale builds back over time.

Septic guidance and permits

According to MassDEP's septic guidance, grease and chemical cleaners should stay out of a septic system and the tank should be pumped on the state's recommended schedule. Clearing a stoppage is exempt from a permit under 248 CMR 3.05.
