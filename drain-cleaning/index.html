Clogged drain emergency: what to do first

If several fixtures are slow at once, or a toilet gurgles when the washing machine drains, the blockage is probably in the main line. Stop running water in the house, because every gallon you add has nowhere to go but the lowest opening, usually a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub.

Skip the bottle of chemical drain opener. It can sit in a standing pipe and burn the next person who works on it, and it rarely moves a root or grease mass. Then call the 24/7 line. If the water is already rising toward an outlet or appliance, say so and keep everyone out of that room.

Signs the clog is more than a slow sink

One fixture drains slowly

Usually hair, soap scum or kitchen grease in the trap or the first run of pipe. A cable or hand auger clears it in one visit.

Several fixtures back up together

That points past the branch lines to the main drain. A basement floor drain that bubbles when you flush is a classic sign.

Gurgling and repeat clogs

A drain that clears and fails again within weeks often has a bellied pipe, roots or a partial collapse. A camera tells you which.

Why Easton drains clog the way they do

Homes near North Easton's village center were plumbed in an era of cast iron and clay, and those materials roughen with age. Rough walls catch grease and paper, and tree roots find the joints. A newer subdivision with PVC drains tends to clog from what goes down them, not from the pipe itself.

Whether your house is on town sewer or a private septic system changes the call. On septic, a main-line backup can mean a full tank or a failed leach field rather than a clog, and a plumber should ask before running a cable. Tell the dispatcher which you have.

What keeps Easton drains from clogging again

Most repeat clogs trace back to a handful of habits. None of them cost money to fix, and a few of them also protect a septic system.

Habits that help

  • Wipe grease and oily pans into the trash before washing, because cooled grease coats the pipe wall and catches everything after it.
  • Keep a hair catcher in every shower and tub drain.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable still pile up in older cast iron lines.
  • Run hot water for half a minute after the dishwasher or sink empties a greasy load.
  • If you notice a slow drain, deal with it that week. A partial clog is cheaper to clear than a full one.

Clear it yourself or call a plumber

A plunger and a hand auger are reasonable first moves on a single fixture. Past that, you are guessing.

You can try first

  • A plunger on a sink, tub or toilet with the overflow blocked
  • Cleaning the trap under a sink with a bucket below
  • A small hand auger on a shower drain full of hair

Call the 24/7 line

  • Two or more fixtures slow at once
  • Water coming up from a floor drain or tub
  • A clog that returns within days
  • Any sewage smell with a backup

If you are not sure which side you are on, call and describe it. The dispatcher will tell you whether it is worth trying anything first.
